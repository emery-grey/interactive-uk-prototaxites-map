Prototaxites & The Secrets Of The Old Red Sandstone

The Giant Mystery of the Silurian and Devonian Landscape

The extinct genus Prototaxites is a large eukaryote from approximately 400 million years ago that grew up to 26 feet high. Long debated whether it would be a plant or fungus, it was recently proposed to be something entirely different, a member of an extinct kingdom.

During the mid-Silurian, what is now considered the UK was underwater, an environment dominated by shallow marine reefs. As the Caledonian orogeny caused major uplift across the area from roughly 490-390 million years ago, the sea retreated and exposed land to the surface. By the early Devonian the landscape was full of terrestrial ecosystems including braided rivers, alluvial fans, and floodplains. This is where the most fossils were found among the rock units, that is described in more detail by clicking on the cards below in the legend.

This data was put together using articles and resources, there was no offical file for prototaxities discovery locations. The rock unit data is from the British Geologic Survey, but the fossil locations are not exact in relation to these units so this is an estimate that requires further study.

Rock Unit Legend

* indicates a wider span of time, which refers to less specific depositional periods.

Silurian Rocks (443–419 Ma)* ▼
Deep to marginal marine sediments, cycles of sediment rising and falling from sea currents. Less specific depositional timeframe; 1 fossil found relative to this unit.
Riccarton Group (434–426 Ma) ▼
Transitional marine to continental, mixed sediments showing a retreat of shallow seas. 1 fossil found relative to this unit.
Wenlock Rocks (433–422 Ma) ▼
Marine reef deposits of limestone, siltstone, mudstone, and greywacke. 3 fossils found relative to this unit.
Ludlow Rocks (427–423 Ma) ▼
Shallow marine to coastal deposits, evidence of lagoons and plant material. Increasing terrestrial influence and oxygenation from the Caledonian orogeny. 1 fossil found relative to this unit.
Pridoli Rocks (423–419 Ma) ▼
Deposits from shallow marine and tidal flats, first evidence of red beds and terrestrial ecosystems. 4 fossils found relative to this unit.
Lower Old Red Sandstone (419–393 Ma)* ▼
Deltas, alluvial fans, and braided rivers characterized this time. Less specific depositional timeframe; 1 fossil found relative to this unit.
Arbuthnott–Garvock Group (416–407 Ma) ▼
Fossil-rich braided river and lake deposits, semi-arid climate with conglomerates, sandstones, and volcanic rocks. Ecosystems influenced by early vascular plants. 5 fossils found relative to this unit.
Upper Devonian Rocks (385–359 Ma)* ▼
Fully established terrestrial ecosystems with rivers, lakes, and floodplains. Less specific depositional timeframe; 5 fossils found relative to this unit.
Old Red Sandstone Supergroup (419–358 Ma)* ▼
Continental basin linked to all red beds from the Silurian to Devonian. Includes rivers, lakes, marine, and desert environments. Red coloration from oxidation. Less specific depositional timeframe; 1 fossil found relative to this unit.
