Paleo Environments During the Time of Prototaxites

Marine

Warm, stable shallow seas with diverse marine life.

Shallow Marine

high sea levels with high inland seas, and high-frequency regression and transgressions evident through sedimentary record.

Terrestrial

The rise of large land plants likely increased atmospheric oxygen and helped create soil, which changed nutrient cycling and potentially fueled Late Devonian marine extinctions The terrestrial landscape was mostly barren, with life restricted to damp coastal regions and wetlands.

Fluvial

Runderwent a major transformation, shifting from predominantly braided, barren systems to meandering, vegetated river systems, heavily influenced by the evolution of land plants, The landscape was largely barren or sparsely covered with small, moss-like plants, meaning little organic stabilization of soils.

Wetlands

Was largely restricted to coastal plains, estuaries, and inland areas around water bodies. These were often brackish or saltwater coastal marshes where early vascular plants were anchored to the substrate by rhizoids, not deep roots.

Deltaic

Following a major extinction, this period saw high sea levels and warm climates. Widespread shallow seas created extensive carbonate platforms with common reef communities involving rugose/tabulate corals and stromatoporoids. Early vascular plants began colonizing coastal lowlands, with initial terrestrial arthropod expansion.
